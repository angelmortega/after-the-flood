    About

    What it explores

    For a place (a ZIP code, city or county) and optionally a storm, it finds out what happened (NOAA storm events and FEMA disaster declarations), what it cost (National Flood Insurance Program claims and payouts, and how often the place floods again), and what came after: did typical home values fall behind similar ZIP codes that the storm barely hit, and did they catch up?

    Who it's for

    People buying or renting a home in a flood-prone area, local journalists, and city-planning students.

    How to use it

    Type a question with a place, or pick an example. If the place is vague, the lead asks you a few questions first. A run takes about a minute. Every number in the report links to the query behind it.

    Data

    • OpenFEMA NFIP Redacted Claims v3 and Disaster Declarations Summaries
    • NOAA NCEI Storm Events Database, from 1996
    • Zillow Home Value Index (ZHVI) by ZIP, from 2000
    • Census ZIP (ZCTA) to county relationships and ZIP locations

    What the data can't tell you

    Whether a specific house flooded (claims are anonymized to ZIP), damage to uninsured homes (few claims can mean few policies), what will happen next (these are records, not forecasts), or why prices moved: the comparison with similar ZIPs is correlation, not causation.
